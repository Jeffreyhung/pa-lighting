# Lighting Consoles

The console is the main control for the brightness of every fixture. By function there are manual, semi-computerised and computerised consoles, differing in how elaborately DC is used to control AC. A console sends one of two kinds of signal — **analog** or **digital** — to operate the dimmer.

## Quick reference

| Console | Type | Channels | Signal cable | Highlights |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Touchlight | Analog | 12 | 25-pin–25-pin | Touch switches, designed for dance parties; HOLD / FLASH modes |
| C-1201 / C-1202 | Analog | 12 | 25-pin–25-pin | Faders, three chasers, sound-to-light |
| C-24X | Digital | 12 / 24 | 3-pin (3-pin–5-pin to the dimmer) | Three modes, 48 scene memories, chases |
| Strand MX | Digital | 12 (up to 512 circuits when patched) | DMX | LCD menus, Scene, Fx, Patch, Autofade |
| DMX console (DMX Operator) | Digital | By fixture | DMX | Directly controls LED PARs and other digital fixtures |
| KK256A (supplement) | Digital | 256 | DMX | Designed for moving lights; 12 fixture pages, 80 scenes |

## Common concepts

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| MASTER | Overall level. Brightness is channel × master (e.g. channel 50%, master 40% → 20%) |
| FLASH | On while held, off when released |
| SCENE | A stored look, recalled with one button |
| CHASE / Fx | Several steps lighting in sequence |
| FADE | Fade-in / fade-out time |
| BLACKOUT | All output stops |
| PATCH | Mapping console channels to dimmer circuits (or fixture DMX addresses) |

!!! warning "Caution"

    Don't dim rotating effect lights with transformers or motors from a console; it damages them.
