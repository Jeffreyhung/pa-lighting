# Stage Lighting Design

## Design thinking

Besides safety, content and programme needs, consider the nature of each fixture to decide what to use and where. PAR-64 is the example: its lens types (N / P / R / S) have different angles and working distances (see Fixtures).

## Four properties of light

| Property | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Intensity | From the faintest the eye can detect to more than it can bear, each level gives a show different life |
| Color | Expresses emotion most directly |
| Distribution | Form and direction; different combinations of light and shadow look different |
| Movement | The timing of fading up and down is the rhythm of the lighting |

## Four purposes of light

1. **Selective visibility**: every level on stage is chosen and designed.
2. **Revelation of form**: form and direction let the picture speak.
3. **Composition**: unify the stage or divide it into areas.
4. **Mood**: create an atmosphere that fits the content.

## Lighting positions

| Position | Location and job | Key points |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Front light** | From in front of the stage; lights the whole stage | Usually PAR-64 N or P from behind the audience; normally no gel, or a pale (colour-correcting) one. Too little and the stage looks grey and video colours dull. Higher than a standing performer: too high shadows the chin, too low throws the mic's shadow on the face; **45° is best** |
| **Side light** | From both sides; fills the sides of the body front light misses, giving a three-dimensional image | Short throw, usually PAR-64 R. Balance with front light: too little is flat, too much leaves bright sides and a dim front. For dance, to stress line, side light is often used alone |
| **Scene (background) light** | On the backdrop, the end of the audience's view, which focuses attention on the performer | Make a flat backdrop three-dimensional by **dividing light into colours and layers** (centre, sides, outermost, top, bottom). E.g. bright yellow centre, earthy orange sides, deep blue outermost → the centre stands out, the sides recede. Also a surface for lasers, moving lights and effects |
| **Back light** | From upstage toward the audience. Separates performer from background, adds depth; also for beam looks | The most stimulating position; the biggest effects happen here. Aim for clear layers, harmonious colour, beam distribution and impact. When focusing: inner lights outward, outer lights inward, top lights down, floor lights up. Effect example: a burst of pure white, high-wattage light slightly above 3200K |
| **Effect light** | Patterns and dots | For dance parties and lively shows |
| **Top light (wash)** | Straight down from above | Blends the whole stage and sets mood; a basic source |
| **Follow spot** | Follows the lead performer, strengthening front light | High colour temperature (3200–5200K); goes blue on video, so use correction gel |

### Key light, fill light, floor light

- **Key light**: above and slightly in front, covering the top of the head that front and side light miss; can also convey setting and time.
- **Fill light**: usually opposite the key, lifting its shadows; not too strong, often in a colour contrasting with the key.
- **Stage floor light**: lights the whole floor to strengthen the overall picture; a Leko set slightly soft works.

## Gels

A gel is a heat-resistant filter. It absorbs most wavelengths and passes only certain ones, which is the colour we see — **subtractive colour**.

Gels differ in transmission: the lower it is, the less light passes and the more fixtures you need.

Combine cool and warm: cool — blue, green; warm — red, orange, yellow.

## Design steps

1. Understand the fixtures
2. Understand the content and programme needs
3. Consider safety and venue conditions
4. Choose fixtures and colours
5. Design positions and rigging
6. Draw up the lighting changes and cue sheet

Consider stage and venue size and throw distances, and combine different fixture types so the stage has a focus. Good lighting presents the director's imagined picture and the script's directions to the audience; observe nature, watch a lot and ask a lot.
