# Load-in & Strike Practice

## Overall sequence

1. **Prepare**: confirm the site plan → estimate total load → check equipment and cables (test them).
2. **Rig**: scaffolding / stands → hang fixtures → run cables.
3. **Tie in power**: only when everything is rigged and cabled (see Power Tie-in Safety & the Multimeter).
4. **Connect console and dimmer, then focus**: fixtures to the dimmer channels you planned; ribbon cable for the Power Pack, signal cable for the Dimmer; then build scenes.
5. **Tidy**: dress cables, fit safeties.
6. **Strike**.

## Scaffolding

A set has H frames, cross (X) braces and planks.

*(Points below are a supplement from the load-in & cabling lecture notes in the folder; the main handout has fill-in blanks here)*

- Put thick cardboard under the feet to protect the floor.
- H frames have a top and a bottom; engage the small latches on the X braces.
- **Two levels at most.**
- Straighten the planks once placed.
- Climb **from the inside**.

## Hanging fixtures (C-clamps)

- A C-clamp fits a scaffold's thick tube or a venue's lighting bar.
- **Fit the light to the bar first, then raise it.**
- Bolt facing inward; never tighten with pliers.
- Cables pointing down.
- Without C-clamps, secure firmly with wire.

## Lighting stands

- Keep them balanced.
- A new T-bar must not go on an old stand; old bars can go higher.
- Don't forget the washers; cables down.
- Fit safeties; leave slack (for raising and lowering) and wrap the cable.

## Safety and safeties

- Every fixture gets a safety: wire on scaffolding or stands; also at the joint with the black cable's connectors (a reef knot; the same for cable-to-cable joints).
- The feeder's safety is tied at the power box; lay the feeder out, don't leave it in a heap.
- Carry a few rolls of electrical tape.

## Running and taping cable

- The console doesn't move, other items may → **no slack at the console end, slack at the item end**.
- **Any cable hanging in the air is an unfinished cable.**
- Keep signal and power apart; mind male/female direction.
- Along the wall, in straight lines: follow an edge, otherwise square corners.
- Seal with electrical tape, firmly.

## Coiling cable

*(Supplement from the load-in & cabling lecture notes)*

- **Over-under coil**: connector end inward, first loop over.
- Tape ties: feeder 4; black/grey and mic cables 3; AC-BAR at head and tail.

## Q&A: lighting

### Q1: What preparation is needed before load-in?

Confirm the site plan → estimate the total load → confirm the equipment is right and no cable is damaged before taking it out. This reduces problems and speeds up the load-in.

### Q2: How do you rig a light well?

Consider the stage and venue size and the throw, and combine different fixture types so the stage has a focus. Gels on PARs strengthen a scene; mix cool (blue, green) and warm (red, orange, yellow).

### Q3: How do you tie in power?

It is the most dangerous step; do it only when everything is rigged and cabled and you are ready to focus. Switch the breaker OFF before connecting, and again before disconnecting. The basic idea: **neutral/ground first, then live; when disconnecting, live first, then neutral/ground.**

### Q4: What are the steps for setting up lighting?

After tie-in, connect fixtures to the channels you planned. The Power Pack needs its ribbon cable to the console to work (the Dimmer also needs a signal cable); then build the looks the event needs.

### Q5: When is a load-in finished?

The hardest and most important part is the final tidy. However messy it got, finish by dressing the cables (along walls, straight lines, taped down) and fitting safeties. Safety is the most important thing.

### Q6: What about the strike?

First switch off the main power at the box, then take down the fixtures. Hot filaments are very fragile: let fixtures cool and avoid knocks. Finally gather everything and count it.

### Notes

1. Whatever the control system, first check that the venue's supply can carry the total wattage, and the maximum load per dimmer channel. Connecting blindly trips breakers or damages fixtures.
2. Breakers don't always have screws; bring your own.
3. If a cable or the feeder gets abnormally hot, stop and switch off; continue when fixed.
4. If fixtures don't respond to the console, check both ends of the ribbon cable and try again.

## Q&A: PA

### Q1: What preparation is needed before load-in?

Confirm the site plan — where sound goes in and comes out; the aim is that listeners hear clearly and comfortably. Prepare cables: which ones, which connectors (RCA, 1/4", XLR, bare wire), how many, and test them.

### Q2: How do you set up speakers well?

Always bolt the speaker to the stand so it can't slide off. Consider distance to listeners, even left/right level and dead spots. Steps are in System Setup under PA System.

### Q3: What do you do about feedback?

Feedback is output and input looping and amplifying endlessly. Turn down the output at the mixer, move the microphone, or cut the feedback frequency with EQ.
